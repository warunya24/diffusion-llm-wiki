Reflection

1. Which node failed most often?

N6 (slide generation), caught by C2. On the first run it produced 13 problems, more than all the other nodes combined (ingest: 2 problems in 7 runs; lint: 1; render: 3 visual issues).

The interesting part is why. Every fact in the wiki was already checked, so the errors were not wrong sources. They appeared when the AI turned wiki facts into teaching text: - It "helped" by doing extra arithmetic ("48× fewer numbers") or converting units ("10–20%"). These numbers look reasonable but appear in no source. - It used vocabulary before teaching it ("forward process" on slide 3, "guidance scale" on the roadmap), because to the AI the words were already familiar. - It cited too few wiki pages on summary slides, so true facts looked unsupported.

The second most error-prone node was N7 (render): the script said PASS, but looking at the screenshots found 3 layout problems.

2. After finding the problem, where did I move the check?

I moved it from after generation (C2) to before generation, at the planning step, by adding a new node:

N5 Fact sheet (tools/make_factsheet.py). Before any slide is written, it reads the slide plan and produces, for every slide: - the exact numeric facts it may use (copied from the wiki pages that slide will cite), - the terms already defined by earlier slides, - the terms that are not yet allowed.

So the generator now sees the boundary while writing, instead of being corrected afterwards. C2 stays as a safety net.

Earlier in the run I had also already moved a check forward on the wiki side: numbers are verified at ingest time against the raw capture (C1), not at the end. That is why the two source conflicts (CLIP "400 million", CFG w vs s) were found during ingest and never reached the slides as errors. Instead they became a teaching point (the ⚠️ box on slide 13).

3. Did I change the workflow / graph? Why?

Yes, from v1 to v2: 1. Added N5 (fact sheet) between the slide plan and generation. Most C2 failures were predictable from the plan alone: which page a slide cites and which terms come later. Checking at the plan stage is cheaper than regenerating slides. 2. Changed the C2 → N6 loop to "regenerate only the failing slides". Regenerating the whole deck could break slides that already passed. 3. Added a human look to N7. The browser script checks errors, overflow and links, but it said PASS while the CFG demo was visually wrong. Some checks can't be automated. 4. Small convention changes found by the checks: placeholders are written as plain text, not bullets (C1 false failure), and step labels are marked nocheck (C2 false positives). A checker that cries wolf gets ignored, so false positives are worth fixing too.

What I learned

  • A grounded knowledge base is not enough. The step that rewrites knowledge for an audience is where hallucination comes back, even with good sources.
  • The cheapest place to catch an error is the earliest node that could have known about it.
  • A PASS only means something if the checker can fail. I tested it with 3 injected faults before trusting it.
