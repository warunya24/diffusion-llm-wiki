Before you start: you only need to know what a neural network and a loss function are.

The big idea: a diffusion model

A diffusion model learns to turn random noise into a realistic image by removing noise a little at a time.

Analogy: drop ink into clear water until it is fully cloudy. A diffusion model is trained to play that movie backwards, from cloudy water back to the clean picture.

It has two halves:

  • Forward process: a fixed recipe that adds noise (nothing to learn).
  • Reverse process: a learned network that removes noise.

Where the name comes from

The idea is inspired by non-equilibrium thermodynamics: things naturally spread out (diffuse) into a mess. The model learns how to undo the mess.

In 2020, DDPM showed this works really well for images (FID 3.17 on CIFAR10, where lower is better).

Step 1 · The forward process: destroying an image on purpose

  • At every step we add a little Gaussian noise (random static).
  • How much noise per step is set by the noise schedule: very little at first, more later.
  • After enough steps (DDPM uses 1000) there is no trace of the picture left, only noise.
  • This part is fixed: there is nothing to learn here.
Analogy: turning up TV static one notch at a time until you can't see the show.
step t = 0  ·  picture kept = 100%

For the curious: the shortcut formula

We don't have to add noise step by step. We can jump straight to any step t in one formula:

xt = √ᾱt · x0 + √(1 − ᾱt) · ε,    ε ~ Gaussian noise

Read it in plain English

  • x₀ = the clean image, xₜ = the noisy image at step t
  • ᾱₜ ("alpha-bar") = how much of the picture is left. It is the product of (1 − β) over all earlier steps.
  • As t grows, ᾱₜ shrinks toward zero → mostly noise.

Two common noise schedules

  • Linear (DDPM): β goes from 1e-4 to 0.02 over T = 1000 steps.
  • Cosine: changes the noise more gently near the start and the end.

Step 2 · How it learns: the "guess the noise" game

  1. Pick a training image.
  2. Pick a random step t (from 1 to T = 1000).
  3. Add a known random noise to make the noisy image.
  4. The network sees the noisy image + t and predicts the noise.
  5. Loss = mean squared error between real noise and predicted noise. Update the weights. Repeat.

This is called noise prediction. One image gives many training examples, because we can add noise at many different amounts.

Analogy: a teacher splashes a known pattern of paint on a photo and asks the student to point out the splash. The teacher made the splash, so the right answer is always known. No human labels needed!
loss = ‖ ε − εθ(xt, t) ‖²

The network inside: a U-Net

The U-Net takes a noisy image and outputs a same-sized "noise map".

  • Down path: shrink the picture to understand what is in it.
  • Up path: grow it back to say where the noise is.
  • Skip connections carry fine details straight across.
  • The same weights are used at every step; the step number t is given as an extra input.

Newer models sometimes use a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) instead.

Step 3 · The reverse process: making a picture from noise

  1. Start with pure random noise.
  2. Ask the network: "what noise is in here?"
  3. Subtract a bit of that predicted noise (and add a tiny bit of fresh noise, except at the last step).
  4. Repeat, from step T down to 1.
Analogy: a sculptor removing a thin layer of stone on each pass. After many passes, a statue appears.

The original DDPM runs all 1000 steps to make one image.

Wait, 1000 steps? That's slow!

The problem

DDPM is trained with T = 1000 steps, and its original sampler uses all of them.

The fix: smarter samplers

  • DDIM can jump along a strided subset of the steps (skip many) and still make good images.
  • With its setting η = 0, it becomes deterministic: same starting noise → same image.
  • So real tools usually use only 50 or 100 sampling steps.
Analogy: walking down a 1000-step staircase vs. taking it several steps at a time. You reach the bottom much faster.

Other speed-up ideas: progressive distillation and consistency models.

Making it fast: work in latent space

Problem: diffusion on full-size pixels is very expensive. Training the strongest pixel models can take hundreds of GPU days.

Idea (Latent Diffusion):

  1. An autoencoder compresses the image into a small grid of numbers (the latent space) that still keeps what humans see.
  2. Run all the denoising steps on that small latent.
  3. The decoder turns the final latent into a full image once at the end.
Analogy: edit a small sketch, and only paint the big canvas at the very end.

Sizes in Stable Diffusion

latent (4, 64, 64) → image (3, 512, 512)

So every denoising step works on a much smaller array than the final picture.

How much to compress?

Compression factor f = 4 to 16 works well; 4 and 8 were best. Too much loses detail, too little is slow.

Reading the prompt: the CLIP text encoder

The text encoder turns your prompt into numbers that capture its meaning.

CLIP (Contrastive Language–Image Pre-training) learned this by playing "which caption belongs to which image?" on 400 million (image, text) pairs from the internet.

  • Matching image + caption → pushed close together.
  • Wrong pairs → pushed far apart.

So words and pictures live in the same vector space.

What comes out

Your prompt → 77 token vectors × 768 numbers each

Analogy: a translator that turns your sentence into the painter's language: lists of numbers.

Known weakness: CLIP is bad at counting objects, which is one reason AI pictures often get "three cats" wrong.

Putting the words into the picture: cross-attention

Cross-attention layers sit between the ResNet blocks of the U-Net.

At each layer, every region of the image can "look at" all the words of the prompt and take in the ones that matter for that spot.

The same trick works for other conditions too, such as layouts / bounding boxes.

Analogy: while painting each part of the canvas, the artist glances at the word list ("red", "cat", "sofa") and picks the word that matters there.

Following the prompt strongly: classifier-free guidance

At each step the model predicts the noise twice: once with the prompt and once with an empty prompt. Then it pushes further in the direction of the difference:

guided = uncond + s · (cond − uncond)
  • s is the guidance scale in tools. s = 1 means no extra push.
  • Bigger s → follows the prompt more closely, but less variety.
  • Training trick: drop the prompt with probability 0.1 or 0.2, so one network learns both "with prompt" and "without prompt".
⚠️ The original paper writes the same rule with w, where s = 1 + w. So the paper's w = 0 is a tool's s = 1.
guidance scale s = 3.0 (try 0, 1, 7)

Remember

Stable Diffusion is not one model. It is a system of several models working together.

Same design, other jobs

Latent diffusion also does inpainting (fill a hole), super-resolution and layout-to-image.

Summary

  • Forward process: add noise on purpose (fixed, no learning).
  • Training: the U-Net learns to guess the added noise (MSE loss).
  • Reverse process: start from noise, subtract predicted noise again and again.
  • DDIM: skip steps → 50 or 100 instead of 1000.
  • Latent space: denoise a small (4, 64, 64) latent, decode once.
  • CLIP text encoder: prompt → 77 × 768 numbers.
  • Cross-attention: image regions read the words.
  • Guidance scale: push toward the prompt, trade variety for accuracy.
